Read a fill from the dealing desk

A dealer sends your fund a message marked Buy. Which balance goes up? Start with the execution below. It represents a single outright spot fill in a fictional bilateral profile. The goal is to go from those exact bytes to the two signed contractual amounts your order management system (OMS) can reconcile. The guide assumes familiarity with Java, decimals and TCP. All rates and identifiers are invented.

Read togetherMeaning in this profile

For the fund, EUR increases by 1,000,000 and USD decreases by 1,100,000. These are value-date obligations. The execution does not establish that cash has already settled. A dealer looking at its own principal book sees the opposite signs. A second feed reporting this same fill may use a different message type, side perspective and ID namespace.

Locate one message in a TCP stream

TagValue encodes each field as a decimal tag number, an equals sign, its value and the one-byte ASCII Start of Heading delimiter (SOH, hex 01). The standard header starts with BeginString (8), BodyLength (9) and MsgType (35), in that order. CheckSum (10) is last. Tags can sit in the header, business body, trailer or a repeating group. Numeric sorting loses structure.

In the report above, start counting at the 3 of 35=8. Count through the SOH immediately before 10=. That span is 164 bytes, including the remainder of the standard header. The checksum sums every byte from the 8 of 8= through that same final SOH, takes the remainder modulo 256, and prints three decimal digits. Here it is 123. Replacing SOH with a pipe preserves byte length in ASCII but changes the checksum.

A socket read may end halfway through tag 9 or return two fills together. Buffer until the first two fields are complete, validate the declared length against the negotiated maximum, wait for that many bytes plus the seven-byte checksum field, validate, dispatch one frame and retain the remainder. A length-prefixed Data field can contain SOH inside the payload. A search for a delimiter or the literal string “10=” is therefore an unsafe framing algorithm. The Python check is for this complete ASCII display fixture only.

A repeated tag can be two different entries

NoPartyIDs (453) counts entries. PartyID (448) opens each one, followed here by the ID source (447) and role (452). The first party is the order origination firm and the second is the executing firm. The dictionary supplies the group delimiter, entry order and nesting. A Map<Integer,String> would silently keep only BANK. In QuickFIX/J, use the generated group type for the enclosing message, getGroup(1, group) for the first entry, and read its fields before reusing the group object.

Turn FX side into signed amounts

Read five things together: the book whose perspective is represented, currency pair, rate units, dealt currency and side. Under the FIX FX recommended practice, Side (54) expresses the initiator buying (1) or selling (2) Currency (15). A firm classified as buy-side can submit either value. A session initiator is the network participant that opens the connection. Its role says nothing about the legs of a particular trade.

Dealt currencySideFill amountClient EURClient USD
EUR1 Buy1,000,000 EUR+1,000,000−1,100,000
EUR2 Sell1,000,000 EUR−1,000,000+1,100,000
USD1 Buy1,100,000 USD−1,000,000+1,100,000
USD2 Sell1,100,000 USD+1,000,000−1,100,000

Let r be USD per EUR and d be the signed dealt amount, positive for a buy. When d is EUR, the EUR leg is d and the USD leg is −d × r. When d is USD, the USD leg is d and the EUR leg is −d ÷ r. Check units before rounding. For either row, EUR × r + USD equals zero before fees and rounding. This catches a multiplication error but cannot catch both signs reversed. Check one leg against the original order instruction too.

Which side of the dealer’s quote?

Suppose the bank quotes EUR/USD 1.0998 bid and 1.1000 offer. Its bid is where it buys EUR. The client selling EUR hits 1.0998 and receives USD 1,099,800 for EUR 1m. The client selling USD 1.1m buys EUR and lifts the 1.1000 offer, receiving EUR 1m. Looking up “Sell → bid” directly on tag 54 selects the wrong price in the second case. First identify whether the client buys or sells the base currency.

Those two encodings produce identical legs at one known fill rate. They specify different orders before a fill. A EUR 1m buy fixes the EUR amount. A USD 1.1m sell fixes the USD amount. At 1.1010 the former costs USD 1,101,000 and the latter buys approximately EUR 999,091.73. Preserve which amount the customer fixed when constructing orders, applying limits and allocating residuals.

Do not infer Currency from the first three letters unless the bilateral profile explicitly supplies that default. PrimeXM, for example, documents that its taker interface accepts base-currency side and size only. Submitting the equivalent USD-denominated instruction there can produce a rejection. If the upstream instruction fixes USD, converting it to a fixed EUR market order changes the execution risk. Reject unsupported semantics or obtain an explicit conversion instruction.

Follow an order through acknowledgement, fills and correction

TransactTime (60) records the business transaction time. SendingTime (52) belongs to a particular transmission. A limit (44) is an instruction. LastPx (31) reports what traded. Sending the order, completing a socket write and receiving a heartbeat each leave the business outcome open. Correlate the venue response to ClOrdID and keep tracking an order even if its first response is already a fill.

Accepted0 / 0— / 0 / 1,000,000Record venue OrderID B17.
First fill E1F / 1400,000 / 400,000 / 600,000Book only 400,000 EUR at its LastPx.
Second fill E2F / 2600,000 / 1,000,000 / 0Book 600,000 EUR. Adding CumQty would overbook the order.

This is an alternative partial-fill scenario. The opening example filled the entire amount in E1. ExecType describes the event, whereas OrdStatus describes the order state after it. A cancel request (F) carries its own ClOrdID and OrigClOrdID (41) referring to the order being cancelled. Cancel/replace (G) creates another link in the request chain. Track the currently accepted order and outstanding requests separately. A fill may cross a cancel in flight. A cancel reject (9) does not cancel the remaining order.

In FIX 4.4 a trade correction (150=G) or trade cancel/bust (150=H) can refer to the affected execution via ExecRefID (19). If E1 was 400,000 EUR at 1.1000 and a correction changes the price to 1.0999, reverse the original USD −440,000 and apply USD −439,960. Net adjustment is USD +40, with no EUR change. Preserve the original, the correction and their link. An execution report with a new ExecID is not automatically another fill. Version-specific event codes matter when migrating older FIX flows.

Connect a gateway and recover a session

Agree the rules of engagement before implementing callbacks. Record environment and endpoints, network route, initiator and acceptor, SenderCompID (49) and TargetCompID (56), protocol and dictionary revision, schedules and time zones, logon authentication, TLS policy, heartbeat interval, message-rate limits, resend retention and test contacts. Order routing, pricing and drop copy often use separate sessions, each with its own sequence space.

With classic FIX the initiator connects and sends Logon (35=A), usually with EncryptMethod (98) and HeartBtInt (108). The acceptor checks the session identity and responds with Logon. Keep inbound expected and outbound next sequence numbers separately across reconnects. Persist messages needed by the negotiated resend policy and fence failover so only one instance owns a session. A bilateral scheduled reset starts a new sequence epoch. An unplanned reset to 1 may hide missing fills. An open-ended resend request uses EndSeqNo=0. Gap-fill advances the session, even when some business content is unavailable under the venue’s resend policy. Establish an independent recovery path for such content.

Uncertain order submission is different from a missing inbound sequence. If the wire broke immediately after F42 was sent, a fresh ClOrdID could place a second real order. Look for F42 on drop copy, reconcile with the broker or use an agreed order-status workflow. Only then decide whether to submit new intent. An engine accepting Session.sendToTarget does not tell you that the venue accepted the order.

Build, parse and normalise the message in Java

Use QuickFIX/J when you want a stateful classic FIX session and generated Java types such as quickfix.fix44.ExecutionReport. The engine owns framing, logon, sequencing and resend. Its dictionary describes fields and groups. Your adapter applies the venue profile and maps the result into an immutable domain object. Artio is another Java choice for latency-sensitive gateways. Its generated encoders and decoders operate on buffers, with an engine/library architecture built on Aeron. Copy values that must outlive a callback. Benchmark the whole path, including journal and booking hand-off, before choosing on latency grounds.

Artio · engine, libraries and generated codecs

For the standalone lab use a JDK 21 and Maven. Put the following two files in one empty directory. The core and generated-message artefacts must have matching versions. The FIX 4.4 dictionary is available on the classpath. This fixture deliberately supplies fixed session headers so that its bytes reproduce exactly. A live session sets those headers itself.

The message factory selects the generated FIX 4.4 class from BeginString and MsgType. The dictionary supplies the structural rules needed to parse groups. Parsing with checksum validation and then calling dd.validate covers different failure modes. Neither step checks whether this counterparty supports the instrument, account or dealt currency. A mismatched version package or generic factory can leave a generic Message where the cast expects an ExecutionReport.

The adapter reads decimal fields as strings into BigDecimal to preserve the wire precision. Converting through double first loses that guarantee. compareTo tests numeric equality across decimal scales. USD division uses an explicit precision for non-terminating results. In booking, retain the reported contra amount where authoritative, record the rate and rounding rules, and round only at the agreed currency and allocation boundary. The lab deliberately handles one pair, normal rate quotation, one trade event and client perspective. Its two reports are alternative fixtures with the same IDs, never consecutive live fills.

Where the code sits in a live engine

Install the application on a SocketInitiator or SocketAcceptor with SessionSettings, a persistent MessageStoreFactory, a LogFactory and the appropriate MessageFactory. Start the engine and observe onLogon before submitting orders. Use Session.sendToTarget(order, sessionID) for outbound application messages. Another dispatch option is MessageCracker.crack(message, sessionID) with typed handlers. The snippet leaves acceptReport to the OMS. Handle other message families negotiated on that session. Route admin messages via fromAdmin/toAdmin, with credential redaction on logon.

For a FIX 4.4 initiator the core settings include ConnectionType=initiator, BeginString=FIX.4.4, SenderCompID, TargetCompID, SocketConnectHost, SocketConnectPort, StartTime, EndTime, HeartBtInt, UseDataDictionary=Y and DataDictionary. Configure store, TLS and schedule for the actual environment. With FIXT use the session transport dictionary plus the negotiated application dictionary. Keep a versioned venue dictionary alongside the mapping. A custom field can be read by tag number. Adding a new repeating group requires structure in the dictionary. Regenerate typed classes if your application needs named accessors for extensions.

Handle settlement dates, forwards, swaps and NDFs

For spot, attach the trade date and contractual value date to both legs. SettlType (63) can describe a convention, and SettlDate (64) supplies an explicit date where the profile permits. Spot dates depend on the currency calendars, holidays and dealing cut-off. “Add two calendar days to SendingTime” can send both cash flows to the wrong date. Preserve the venue-confirmed date and flag discrepancies against the calendar service.

Keep replay and booking consistent

Suppose the fund stores E1, posts the EUR and USD obligations, then crashes before its inbound sequence checkpoint is durable. On replay the message can arrive again. Make the execution identity unique within the venue-defined namespace, often including firm, feed or business date. Record the source event, both accounting entries and the downstream outbox record in one database transaction. A repeated identity with matching economic payload produces no second posting. If it has different terms, investigate correction semantics or an ID collision. A raw-byte comparison would see legitimate resend header differences.

The opposite crash window is more dangerous: the engine advances its inbound state before the OMS has durably accepted the event. Verify the engine/store/callback ordering for your deployment and test a kill at this boundary. A durable inbound journal with a separate booking cursor lets you resume business processing independently. If the journal has a gap, ask for supported business replay or reconcile trade capture and broker records. Kafka offsets downstream concern a different stream. Record the relationship between source report, normalised event and posting IDs.

Certification evidence to bring back from the venue

Request paired order and report examples for base and terms amounts, or an explicit rejection for the unsupported form. Test a fill while cancel is pending, a corrected trade, an expired quote, a disconnect after send and a replay across a business-date boundary. Capture raw frames securely, session sequence checkpoints, execution IDs and the ledger rows they generated. For each case the desk should be able to name the surviving order, signed amounts, date and owner. A green Logon tests only the beginning of that chain.
